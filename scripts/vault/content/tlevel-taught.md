## Core Paper 1

### 01 — Problem solving (units 1.1, 1.2, 1.3)
- **1.1.1–1.1.2 Pattern recognition** — definition and purpose of computational thinking; spotting trends, similarities and common features between problems and existing solutions; making predictions from identified patterns.
- **1.1.3 Benefits & drawbacks of computational thinking** — faster, more consistent problem solving and reusable solutions vs oversimplification, assumption drift, and time spent structuring small problems.
- **1.1.4 Components of computational thinking** — decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, algorithmic design; benefits and drawbacks of each.
- **1.1.6–1.1.10 Decomposition** — purpose; tasks (identify main features, characterise them, break problem and solution into manageable parts); representation methods: block diagrams, information flow diagrams, flowcharts, written descriptions.
- **1.2.1–1.2.3 Algorithms and flowcharts** — characteristics and purpose of algorithms; how they work; representing them with flowcharts and pseudocode; standard control structures: sequence, selection, iteration (count-controlled and condition-controlled).
- **1.2.5–1.2.10 (planned in vault, not yet taught)** — actions controlling ordering of steps; purpose of an algorithm; designing algorithms and solutions.

### 02 — Introduction to digital support (unit 2.1–2.11, taught subset)
- **2.10.1 Kolb's learning cycle** — concrete experience → reflective observation → abstract conceptualisation → active experimentation; purpose of reflective learning for a support professional.
- **2.10.2 Gibbs' reflective cycle** — description, feelings, evaluation, analysis, conclusion, action plan; how it structures continuous improvement.

### 03 — Data (vault plan; partially GCSE CS in a Day)
- 3.1–3.8 of the GCSE recap cover algorithms, programming, data representation, computer systems, networks, cyber security, databases/SQL, ethics law and environment.

## Core Paper 2

### 04 — Legislation and regulatory requirements (taught: 4.1.1–4.1.6)
- **4.1.1 Health & Safety** — HSWA 1974 duties of employers and employees; DSE workstation assessments; risks: posture, repetitive strain, eye strain, trips over cabling; reporting and training duties.
- **4.1.2 Data Protection Act 2018 / UK GDPR** — data subject, data controller, data processor; seven principles (lawfulness fairness transparency; purpose limitation; data minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; integrity confidentiality; accountability); lawful bases; subject access requests; ICO; fines.
- **4.1.3 Computer Misuse Act 1990 & malware/threats** — three offences (unauthorised access; access with intent to commit further offence; unauthorised modification with intent to impair); viruses, worms, trojans, ransomware, spyware, adware; phishing and social engineering.
- **4.1.4 Equality Act 2010** — nine protected characteristics; direct and indirect discrimination; harassment and victimisation; reasonable adjustments for digital services; accessibility.
- **4.1.5 Intellectual Property Act 2014 & IP** — copyright, trademarks, patents, design rights; software licensing (proprietary, open source, freeware, shareware); consequences of infringement.
- **4.1.6 Workplace monitoring** — lawful monitoring (which includes informing workers), GDPR and human-rights limits, productivity/security justifications vs privacy, covert monitoring only in exceptional circumstances.

### 05–08 (vault plan; not yet taught — business context, emerging issues, digital environments 7.1–7.2 partially via Pete, security)
- **7.1.1 Physical computers** — the four main categories of computer; input/output; internal components.
- **7.1.2 Hardware devices** — CPUs, storage (RAID, SAN vs NAS), peripherals, XOR logic gates, connectivity hardware.
- **7.2.1 Operating systems** — purpose, features, functions, types.
- **7.2.2 Common utilities** — anti-virus, firewalls, backup, compression, disk tools.
- **7.2.3 Common applications** — word processors, spreadsheets, databases, presentation, email, browsers: features and uses.
